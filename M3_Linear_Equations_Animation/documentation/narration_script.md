> "Imagine being given two equations with two unknowns: 2x plus y equals 5, and x minus y equals 1. How do we represent both equations together in a single mathematical expression? ## Scene 2: Concept Introduction (00:25 – 01:00)
*Target Duration: 35.0s*

> "A system of linear equations contains multiple conditions that must be satisfied simultaneously. In our system, x and y are the unknown variables. The numbers multiplying them—2, 1, and negative 1—are the coefficients. And the numbers on the right-hand side—5 and 1—are constants. Matrix notation unifies this entire system into the compact equation A x equals b. A is the coefficient matrix, x is the column vector of variables, and b is the column vector of constants. For a general system of m equations and n unknowns, their dimensions fit together precisely as m by n, n by 1, and m by 1."

## Scene 3: Visual Intuition & Matrix Construction (01:00 – 01:55)
*Target Duration: 55.0s*

> "Now, let's construct the matrix equation step by step from our example. In the first equation, 2x plus 1y equals 5, the coefficients of x and y are 2 and 1. We extract them directly into the first row of matrix A. In the second equation, 1x minus 1y equals 1, the coefficients are 1 and negative 1. These form the second row. Together, they form our 2 by 2 coefficient matrix A. Next, we collect the unknowns into the column vector x, and the constants into the column vector b. When we multiply matrix A by vector x, the first row recreates our first equation: 2x plus y equals 5. The second row recreates our second equation: x minus y equals 1. The matrix equation is not a different mathematical problem—it is our exact system in a compact algebraic representation."

## Scene 4: Worked Example & Verification (01:55 – 03:35)
*Target Duration: 100.0s*

> "Now, let's solve the system algebraically. We start with equation one: 2x plus y equals 5, and equation two: x minus y equals 1. We can eliminate y by adding both equations. Notice how the positive y and negative y cancel out cleanly. This leaves 3x equals 6. Dividing both sides by 3, we find x equals 2. Now, we substitute x equals 2 back into the second equation: 2 minus y equals 1. Subtracting 2 from both sides gives negative y equals negative 1, which means y equals 1. Our unique solution is x equals 2 and y equals 1, forming the solution vector: column vector 2, 1. In mathematics, we should always verify our solution. Let's substitute our solution vector into the matrix equation A x. We multiply the coefficient matrix by the vector 2, 1 using row by column multiplication. For the first row: 2 times 2 plus 1 times 1 gives 4 plus 1, which equals 5. For the second row: 1 times 2 minus 1 times 1 gives 2 minus 1, which equals 1. The result is the constant vector 5, 1, exactly matching vector b. A x equals b is fully satisfied, proving our algebraic solution is completely correct."

## Scene 5: Geometric Interpretation & Solution Sets (03:35 – 04:05)
*Target Duration: 30.0s*

> "Geometrically, each linear equation represents a straight line on the Cartesian plane. Solving the system means finding where the two lines intersect. Rearranging our equations gives y equals 5 minus 2x, and y equals x minus 1. Plotting both lines, they intersect at the unique point (2, 1), exactly matching our matrix solution vector. More generally, linear systems have three possible geometric outcomes: Intersecting lines give one unique solution. Parallel lines never meet, giving no solution. And coincident lines overlap everywhere, giving infinitely many solutions."
